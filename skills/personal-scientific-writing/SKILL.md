# Personal Scientific Writing

Apply the user's writing preferences below to the requested manuscript work. Treat the latest supplied manuscript, data, figures, tables, and author corrections as the project record. This skill supplies writing and verification instructions; it does not supply scientific evidence or authorize new analyses.

## Personal requirements

1. Use APA 7th edition for in-text citations and the reference list. Preserve this choice unless the user explicitly requests another style. Flag a conflicting journal requirement without silently changing the style. Read [APA and verification](references/apa-and-verification.md) when adding, editing, or auditing citations.
2. Avoid excessive reuse of a reference. Aim to cite a work once per paragraph, using a clearly attributed, coherent passage. Consolidate redundant statements and source clusters before considering another citation. Across paragraphs, repeat a source only when it supports a distinct necessary point. Never substitute a less relevant reference just for variety, or remove attribution needed to identify the source.
3. Every substantive scientific statement must be supported by the project record or an authentic, relevant cited source. This includes opening background statements, geographic descriptions, general biological claims, interpretations, mechanisms, and statements about significance. An unsupported statement must be omitted or narrowed to a supported statement. Adding “may” or “suggests” does not repair missing evidence.
4. Use dashes and quotation marks sparingly. Prefer ordinary sentence structure over parenthetical em dashes and rhetorical quotation marks. Preserve required hyphens, numeric ranges, minus signs, sequence identifiers, exact titles, and necessary quotations. Do not alter a reference title merely to remove punctuation.
5. Keep subheadings concise and informative. Prefer short topic phrases, usually two to six words, with parallel wording at the same level. This is a preference, not a rigid word limit. Avoid sentence-length headings, compound inventories, and unnecessary nesting.
6. Verify both reference authenticity and relevance. Confirm publication metadata and inspect the source passage, figure, or table that supports the actual claim. A valid DOI or a matching title alone is insufficient.
7. Build literature depth, not just citation density. Combine relevant recent primary studies with foundational work where it establishes a mechanism, concept, or historical comparison. When asked for many references, expand the evidence search and include additional distinct support; do not pad the bibliography or impose an arbitrary reference quota. Read [Discussion synthesis](references/discussion-synthesis.md) when drafting or substantially revising a Discussion.

Evidence support and clear attribution take priority when reducing repetition or shortening prose. These citation-density preferences are the user's preferences, not APA rules.

## Establish the evidence

Read the relevant current project files before drafting. Identify the requested section, scope, word target, and approved results. Do not infer file contents from their names or rely on an earlier conversational summary when the underlying file is available.

For substantial Methods or Results work, read [Methods, results and source data](references/methods-results-and-source-data.md). Establish the current figure, table and dataset release before inserting cross-references. When the user prioritizes supplementary-data assembly or figure revision and puts drafting on hold, complete that deliverable without silently rewriting the manuscript. After verification, update only the authorized text and affected cross-references; a new supplementary table does not by itself require a complete rewrite.

For substantial drafting or citation audits, keep a compact evidence ledger outside the manuscript. For a small language edit, use the same checks without producing an unnecessary separate artifact. Classify scientific claims as:

- **Project finding:** trace to the supplied Results, data, figure, table, or documented procedure. Use an internal pointer when it helps the reader. Do not require an external citation merely to report the study's own observation.
- **Literature statement:** trace to a verified source and provide a citation at a location where its scope is clear.
- **Interpretation:** trace the observed pattern to project evidence and cite verified literature for the proposed explanation. Separate the observation from the interpretation, retain uncertainty, and do not claim the mechanism was tested unless it was.
- **Unsupported or unresolved:** omit from finished prose and record the missing evidence separately if it materially affects the requested section.

Split sentences that combine supported observations with unsupported explanations. A reference cannot establish that this project performed a procedure or obtained a result. A study objective, organizational transition, or accurate description of supplied material does not need an invented external citation. A scientific rationale embedded in such a sentence does.

Preserve numerical values, sample labels, units, statistical results, accession numbers, methodological details, and gene names. Do not invent missing Methods details, significance tests, differences between groups, or publication metadata. Correct a documented inconsistency transparently and report any change that affects interpretation.

## Verify before citing

Use available scholarly databases, publisher records, and source documents. Read the verification procedure in [APA and verification](references/apa-and-verification.md). Existing references supplied by the user remain candidates until the relevant checks have been performed or a usable verification record is available.

Prefer relevant recent primary research when the task calls for recent literature. Retain older foundational or method papers when they are the appropriate evidence. A recent date, prestigious journal, or high citation count does not establish relevance.

For unavailable full text, cite only a claim explicitly supported by the accessible abstract, and record that access limit. Do not attribute detailed Methods, numerical results, or figure findings that were not inspected. If verification is unavailable, complete supported portions and report the remaining limitation; never label unchecked references verified.

Do not add hypothetical explanations merely to make a Discussion longer. Include a possible explanation only when both the project pattern and the explanatory literature support a cautious connection. If the user has excluded hypotheses, omit speculative explanations altogether.

## Draft and revise

Write connected scientific prose. Give each paragraph a clear point supported by evidence and an appropriate interpretation. Avoid generic importance claims, promotional language, repetitive transitions, and explanations of the writing process inside the manuscript.

Use the actual section's purpose:

- **Introduction:** establish a cited scientific context and specific gap, then state the study's documented scope and objectives. Keep project advantages proportionate to the design. Avoid unsupported first-ever or literature-absence claims.
- **Materials and Methods:** explain the procedures actually performed and the choices needed to reproduce them. Use equations where they clarify transformations, objectives, validation or metrics, with symbols defined at first use. A companion paper can document shared sampling methods, but does not make an unpublished draft a required citation or justify presenting a new analysis as an extension of that paper. Preserve appropriate attribution to original methods and software.
- **Results:** develop the scientific argument through complementary findings, with accurate references to finalized figures, supplementary tables and datasets. Distinguish descriptive summaries from inference and observation from mechanism. Retain foundational genomic and ecological findings when they support a machine-learning-led paper, and test the central contribution against the strongest completed comparisons.
- **Discussion:** compare the study's actual findings with relevant literature; account for differences in habitats, samples, methods, and uncertainty. Explain differences only as far as the evidence allows. Avoid merely repeating Results.
- **Abstract and Conclusion:** summarize supported findings and bounded implications. Introduce no new results or unsupported background. Where the requested format avoids citations, use project-supported content and omit external claims that would require citations.

Keep legitimate hedging, conventional passive Methods prose, precise terminology, and useful sentence-length variation. Do not force synonyms for technical terms or add personality, invented details, or artificial mistakes to make prose sound human. Improve the argument before polishing its rhythm.

When working on Antarctic viral metagenomics or AMGs, read [Antarctic project context](references/antarctic-project.md). Do not apply that project's facts or exclusions automatically to other projects.

## Final audit and delivery

Before delivery, check:

- Every scientific assertion has identifiable project evidence or a verified citation; broad opening sentences receive the same scrutiny as detailed claims.
- Each citation supports its precise clause, with no unsupported transfer across species, environments, methods, or evidence types.
- Major interpretive themes have adequate literature depth, including directly comparable studies and foundational evidence where relevant; citation counts alone are not treated as evidence of completeness.
- Repeated citations within paragraphs have been consolidated where attribution remains clear; no reference has been added solely to meet a diversity quota.
- In-text citations and reference entries correspond, authors and years match, DOI/metadata are accurate, and APA 7 formatting is consistent.
- Claim strength matches the design and analysis; correlations, predictions, annotations, and demonstrated mechanisms remain distinct.
- Methods contain the performed procedures and necessary mathematical definitions. Internal file paths, transcription audits, failed exploratory runs and drafting commentary stay in project records unless a specific item is scientifically necessary to report.
- Figure, table, dataset and accession references match the current verified release. Related unpublished manuscripts are handled through the appropriate disclosure process, not automatically inserted as published evidence.
- Dashes and quotation marks serve a necessary purpose; subheadings are short; no unsupported novelty, hype, or redundant project-advantage claims remain.
- The requested length, language, section scope, and output format are satisfied without removing evidence or necessary qualification.

Deliver publication-style prose and the relevant APA reference list. Keep evidence ledgers, omissions, unresolved verification, and material editorial changes outside manuscript prose. Provide only the audit detail needed for the task; do not append tables to a manuscript unless requested. Default to Markdown when no other format is specified. If the user requests only prose, report a material blocker briefly outside the draft rather than concealing it.
