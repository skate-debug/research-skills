# APA and verification

Read when adding, revising, or auditing manuscript citations. The user's requirement for comprehensive scientific support and restrained repetition is stricter than a general formatting rule.

## APA 7 essentials

Use author–date citations. For two authors, use “and” in narrative text and “&” in parentheses. For three or more, use the first surname plus “et al.” unless ambiguity requires additional names. Order multiple works in a parenthetical group alphabetically and separate them with semicolons. Distinguish otherwise identical author/year citations with the appropriate APA disambiguation, keeping suffixes consistent with the reference list. Attribute paraphrased passages clearly; citations need not follow every sentence when the source remains unambiguous. Consult [Purdue OWL: Author/Authors](https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/research_and_citation/apa_style/apa_formatting_and_style_guide/in_text_citations_author_authors.html) for these conventions.

For journal articles, write authors' surnames and initials, year, sentence-case article title, italicized journal title and volume, issue in parentheses, pages or article number, and DOI as an HTTPS DOI URL when assigned. Preserve proper nouns and scientific nomenclature. Do not guess absent fields or truncate author names merely to shorten an entry. Consult [Purdue OWL: Articles in Periodicals](https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/research_and_citation/apa_style/apa_formatting_and_style_guide/reference_list_articles_in_periodicals.html) and the [APA journal-article examples](https://apastyle.apa.org/style-grammar-guidelines/references/examples/journal-article-references) for unusual cases.

Alphabetize the reference list and use hanging indents in formats that support them. Standard Markdown can preserve ordering and italics but does not reliably encode hanging indentation. Check the current APA rule for long author lists, translated works, datasets, software, preprints, or other less common source types rather than improvising.

Prefer paraphrase. If a necessary direct quotation is used, preserve its wording and provide the required page or alternative locator. Do not strip quotation marks required by APA merely to satisfy the user's preference for fewer quotation marks.

## Authenticity and claim support

For each source entering the manuscript:

1. Resolve the DOI, PMID, or publisher record. Match title, author order, year, journal, volume/issue, pages or article number to the actual publication. Cross-check conflicting metadata with another authoritative record. A source without a DOI can still be authentic.
2. Check available publisher correction, retraction, and version information. Prefer the corrected version of record where appropriate. Identify preprints explicitly and look for a published successor. Do not use a retracted result as reliable support.
3. Read the relevant source material. Record the page, section, figure, or table and a brief evidence paraphrase. Use a short quotation only if exact wording is needed and quotation limits permit it. Evidence may reside in a table or figure; it need not be a verbatim sentence.
4. Match the claim's population, habitat, sample type, assay, analytical method, outcome, direction, magnitude, and uncertainty to the source. Topic similarity alone is insufficient.
5. Check the strength of support. An association cannot support a causal assertion. Gene annotation cannot establish expression or metabolic activity. Evidence from a different environment must be introduced explicitly as comparison or context.
6. Use the supported wording, narrow a partially supported claim, or remove it. Do not rescue a contradicted pairing with vague hedging. If removal changes the manuscript's central conclusion, flag that consequence to the author.

Use separate fields for bibliographic verification and claim support. A compact working ledger may contain: claim ID/location; proposed wording; project pointer; source identifier; metadata status; access level; supporting locator; support status; action.

Suggested support statuses are **supported**, **supported after narrowing**, **unsupported**, **contradicted**, and **unresolved**. Unavailable evidence is unresolved, not automatically false. Only supported wording enters finished prose. Keep unresolved items outside it.

## Repetition review

Read citation scope at the paragraph level. Introduce a source where the borrowed idea begins, then consolidate closely related findings into a clearly attributed passage. Avoid a single citation at the end of a long paragraph that appears to support unrelated claims. If attribution becomes ambiguous, restructure first and repeat the citation only when necessary for clarity.

Across a section, distinguish repeated citation of a useful source from repeated presentation of the same idea. Remove the latter. Include additional independent studies when they contribute relevant evidence or comparison, not to satisfy a numerical quota. No fixed whole-paper citation cap applies.

## Verification limits

Search snippets, AI summaries, reference-manager imports, and previous assistant outputs are discovery aids, not proof of source content. Do not present an uninspected primary article as though it has been read. Do not describe a check as complete merely because a citation-formatting tool accepted it.

A claim already verified against an identified version can reuse that evidence record if its wording and scope remain supported. Recheck publication status when a recent update or final submission audit makes it relevant.
