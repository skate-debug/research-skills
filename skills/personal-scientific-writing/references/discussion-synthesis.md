# Discussion synthesis

Use for substantive Discussion drafting and revision. Follow the latest requested structure; these principles do not prescribe a universal heading scheme or word count.

## Develop comparisons

For each major result or functional theme, seek the closest comparison by habitat, organism, sample material, assay, and outcome. Prefer direct comparisons over generic reviews. If no close comparison is verified, say what the project establishes rather than imply agreement with an unrelated study.

Develop the argument through the project finding, the relevant published finding, their agreement or difference, and its supported meaning. Do not force this into an identical sentence template. Where several distinct aspects are discussed, seek complementary independent evidence instead of making one loosely related paper support the entire paragraph.

Explain differences with documented design or analytical differences when available. Biological explanations require both a project pattern and literature supporting the connection. State whether an explanation is demonstrated or an evidence-grounded interpretation. Do not treat adding “may” as sufficient support. Respect a project-specific exclusion of speculative hypotheses.

## Balance the literature

Recent research establishes the current comparative picture; foundational work supplies original discoveries, experimental mechanisms, and long-term context. Neither replaces the other. Use primary experimental studies for mechanistic claims where available, observational studies for ecological comparisons, and authoritative reviews for synthesis. Do not impose age cutoffs unless requested.

Separate a demonstrated mechanism in a reference system from a candidate mechanism in the user's dataset. A classic cyanophage experiment, for example, cannot validate an Antarctic gene annotation or imply that an unreported host or pathway was found.

When asked to maximize references, broaden coverage across the actual themes before increasing citations per sentence. Add a source only if its distinct contribution can be stated. Remove redundant source clusters, off-topic prestige citations, and references included solely to raise the count.

## Audit support and emphasis

Classify sentences as project findings, external knowledge, interpretations, or limitations. Project findings normally need project figure/table pointers, not external citations. Biological functions, general methodological explanations, and ecological mechanisms need relevant literature. Study-specific arithmetic, sampling limitations, and accurate summaries of the documented analysis do not automatically require external references.

Assess citation adequacy by claim coverage and comparative depth, not by the proportion of sentences ending in parentheses. Cite close enough to establish scope. Retain the preference for one citation of a given work per paragraph without allowing attribution to become ambiguous.

Avoid a repeated pattern of result, one distant comparison, and several disclaimers. Give the supported ecological meaning sufficient space. Consolidate shared limitations in a short synthesis passage, keeping a local qualification when omitting it would materially overstate that specific finding. Do not remove uncertainty merely to sound more decisive.

Keep evidence ledgers, missing-source notices, unresolved data problems, and editorial explanations outside publication prose. State evidence-access limits honestly. A substantial revision should record the role of added sources and verify that every bibliography entry is cited and every citation has an entry.
